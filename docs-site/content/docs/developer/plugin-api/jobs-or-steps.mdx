title: Step by step or as a job
description: Who runs the steps of a task, the assistant or XIV MCP, and what each costs.

A **long-running tool** is one tool that takes a while, such as `run_duty`. A **job** is a list of tool calls that XIV MCP runs by itself. Any task with several steps can be run either way. The difference is who runs the steps: the assistant, or XIV MCP.

## Step by step

The assistant calls a tool, reads the result, and picks the next call.

<Mermaid
  caption="Every call, and every check while waiting, is a request to the AI model."
  chart={`sequenceDiagram
  actor P as Player
  participant A as Assistant
  participant X as XIV MCP
  P->>A: Flag 21.4, 18.2 and tell me when I am there
  A->>X: set_map_flag x 21.4, y 18.2
  X-->>A: flag set, 310 yalms away
  loop until the player is there
    A->>X: get_position
    X-->>A: 120 yalms to the flag
  end
  A-->>P: You are there`}
/>

- It adapts to every result: full bags, a missing NPC, a price that moved.
- It is expensive. Each call is a model request, and waiting means asking again and again.
- Each call has to finish within the AI app's [time limit](/docs/developer/plugin-api/time-limits), often a minute, and the chat has to stay open.

## As a job

The assistant sends the whole list once. XIV MCP runs the steps, passes results from one to the next, and needs no model while it does.

<Mermaid
  caption="XIV MCP works through the steps itself. The assistant only starts the job and looks in when it wants to."
  chart={`sequenceDiagram
  actor P as Player
  participant A as Assistant
  participant X as XIV MCP
  participant G as Game
  P->>A: Craft collectables and turn them in
  A->>X: start_job with 4 steps
  X-->>A: job started
  rect rgba(59, 239, 196, 0.14)
    Note over X,G: XIV MCP runs every step itself, no model involved
    X->>G: 1. prepare_craft_plan
    G-->>X: plan, list 7
    X->>G: 2. gather_until (40 min)
    G-->>X: materials in the bags
    X->>G: 3. run_crafting_list, list 7 (25 min)
    G-->>X: 20 crafted
    X->>G: 4. turn_in_collectables
    G-->>X: 2,400 scrips
  end
  A->>X: get_job
  X-->>A: done`}
/>

- It is cheap. Starting costs one request; the steps cost none.
- Steps can take hours, and the chat can be closed.
- It follows the plan and cannot decide anything on its own. If a step fails, the job waits as *pending* until someone retries, skips or changes it.

## Side by side

| | Step by step | As a job |
|---|---|---|
| **Who runs the steps** | The assistant | XIV MCP |
| **Model requests** | One per call and per check | One to start, plus any checks |
| **Surprises** | Handled after every step | The step fails, the job waits |
| **Long steps** | Within the app's [time limit](/docs/developer/plugin-api/time-limits) | Hours |
| **The chat** | Must stay open | Can be closed |

Both follow the same permission settings.

## Which one to use

<Mermaid
  caption="A rule of thumb."
  chart={`flowchart LR
  T(["A task with<br/>several steps"]) --> L{{"A step takes longer than<br/>the app waits for a call?"}}
  L -- yes --> J["As a job"]
  L -- no --> K{{"Plan known<br/>before it starts?"}}
  K -- yes --> J
  K -- "no, each step depends<br/>on what happened" --> S["Step by step"]`}
/>

They mix well: look around step by step, hand the long stretch to a job, then decide what to do with its result.

## For your plugin

- Make long-running tools safe to stop and to run again, and return results the next step can use. See [Long-running tools](/docs/developer/plugin-api/long-running).
- Fail with what is left ("3 Iron Ore still missing"), so whoever fixes the pending step knows what to do.
- Jobs your plugin starts have no assistant watching. Check `GetJob(id)`, and cancel a *pending* job or tell the player. See [Starting jobs](/docs/developer/plugin-api/jobs#permissions-and-control).
- Offer quick, read-only status tools, so checks are cheap when the assistant drives.
